Dubai travel guide/Ras Al Khaimah

Ras Al Khaimah: mountain views and the old coast

Give Jebel Jais its own outing, then use a second day for the coast and a heritage stop. Ras Al Khaimah is spread out, so a list of nearby-looking pins can become a long driving day.

Reviewed 30 October 2026

Jebel Jais: go for the mountain, not a checklist

The Jebel Jais road leads to viewpoints and managed activities. A viewing-deck visit and a zipline booking are different experiences with different requirements. Check the current operator page before making the drive.

Our recommendation is a daylight mountain outing in the cooler months, with enough time to stop at designated viewpoints and return without rushing. It can be cooler and windier at elevation. A road viewpoint does not provide access to every ridge or to the summit.

Dhayah Fort and Jazirat Al Hamra

Dhayah Fort gives a hilltop view over its surrounding landscape. The approach involves steps and uneven ground; it is not a level-access stop. Visit in daylight, with the cooler part of the day preferable for the climb.

Jazirat Al Hamra preserves buildings from the old pearling settlement. Stay on open visitor paths and avoid entering fragile or closed structures. It is a good place to slow down and look at building materials and layout, rather than treating the village as an abandoned-site playground.

Leave room for the coast

A quieter waterfront meal or a booked heritage experience can round out a coastal day. Choose one end of the coast rather than driving between every beach and fort. Wadi Shawka is a separate inland option if a marked walk interests you more than the mountain road.

Our two-week itinerary uses two nights in Ras Al Khaimah. On the way south, Umm Al Quwain and Ajman offer mangroves and smaller-scale coastal stops.
