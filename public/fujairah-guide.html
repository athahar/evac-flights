Fujairah: forts, the east coast and mountain roads

Use Fujairah as a base for the UAE’s east coast. Spend one day on heritage stops and another on the coast or a managed activity, rather than driving back to Dubai each evening.

Reviewed 30 October 2026

Start with the fort and museum

Fujairah Fort stands above the older part of the city. The nearby museum adds context through archaeology and local heritage displays. Choose current opening times before planning to see both, and leave the exposed fort area for a cooler part of the day where possible.

Al Hail is a smaller inland heritage stop in a mountain setting. Confirm access before making it the main purpose of a drive. It offers a different scale from the city fort, but it is not a reason to enter closed buildings or take an unsuitable road.

Al Bidya and the Gulf of Oman

Al Bidya Mosque is a small historic place of worship. Dress respectfully, follow local visiting arrangements and remember that it is not simply a photo backdrop. Combine the stop with a coastal journey rather than crossing the emirate for a few hurried minutes.

For swimming, snorkelling or diving, use an appropriate beach or licensed operator and check the day’s sea conditions. A calm-looking photograph does not describe the water on your travel date.

Two nights make the east coast worthwhile

Khorfakkan and Al Rafisah are nearby regional options, though Khorfakkan belongs to Sharjah. Group stops by their location rather than their emirate label. A two-night base lets you enjoy the coast without moving hotels after every outing.

Do not include Wadi Wurayah as an unrestricted hiking or swimming stop. Our wadi guide explains its protected access and suggests other options. The two-week route links the east coast with Al Ain and Ras Al Khaimah, with daylight travel between bases.
