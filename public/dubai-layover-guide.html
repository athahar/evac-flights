A Dubai layover: should you leave the airport?

A layover length alone is not enough to decide. Confirm entry eligibility, baggage arrangements and your next check-in or boarding deadline before planning time outside the airport.

Reviewed 30 October 2026

Count usable time, not the gap between flights

Start with the time you must be back for your next flight. Subtract your return journey, airport procedures, the trip into the city and time to get off the first flight. What remains is your sightseeing window.

A flight arriving late or a long queue can consume that window. Keep a plan that still works if you stay at the airport. There is no universal number of hours that guarantees a city visit will fit.

One booking and separate tickets are different

Ask your airline whether your bags are checked through and where you receive the next boarding pass. On separate tickets, collecting bags and checking in again may require passing through immigration. Do not assume that staying “in transit” resolves this.

Dubai Airports directs connecting passengers to transfer desks for boarding-pass assistance. Its transfer guidance also explains baggage arrangements and terminal connections. Confirm your own itinerary with the airline.

If a city visit fits, choose one area

Pick one neighbourhood and one main activity. An airport connection is a poor time to combine Old Dubai, Downtown and the Marina in a single outing.

Check the attraction’s opening hours against your actual window. An overnight stop and a daytime stop need different plans. A meal and a rest may serve you better than a rushed tour.

Four things to confirm

  1. Your eligibility to enter the UAE, using official guidance and your airline.
  2. Whether baggage must be collected or can remain checked through.
  3. The correct airport, terminal and check-in deadline for the next flight.
  4. A return plan with enough margin for delays.
